# Charter — what we collect, what we grade, what we owe you

This is the deal behind `agent-sessions/` and the sprint reports, in writing. If anything
here stops being true, say so — loudly.

**Acknowledgment.** This Charter binds through your own act: your first commit in this
repo adds a line under the README Roster — `@your-handle — Charter read at <short-sha>,
<date>`. Nobody may add it for you. Research-corpus opt-in (anything beyond §1's closed
list) is a separate, default-no choice — add `research corpus: yes` to your line only if
you mean it, and revoke it anytime by another commit.

## 1. Your logs

**What is collected:** raw agent-session logs and their summaries, in this repo.

**Who reads them:** your team; practitioners — volunteers from a partner company and KSE
staff; and the digest agent, an LLM operated by practitioners; the digest agent reads your
summaries and commit metadata — never your raw logs by default (cohort repo `docs/HITL.md`
rule 7) — and runs under enterprise API terms that do not train on your content; the
provider is named in your cohort repo.

**What they may be used for** — and this list is closed:

1. Teaching and unblocking you.
2. Assessment, exactly as §2 describes.
3. The cohort digest.
4. Improving the program itself — as team-level aggregates inside the org, never
   individual-level analytics; anything that leaves the cohort boundary (including
   cross-program dashboards) is "anything else" below and needs your opt-in.

Anything else — research publication, model training, marketing, individual-level
cross-cohort analytics — requires your separate opt-in, asked per use.

You may **redact personal content from a raw log before committing it**, provided the
summary stays truthful. Redaction or deletion requests go to the cohort data steward — by
default the KSE course lead named in your cohort repo's README: a KSE staff member, never
a partner-company volunteer, because the person deciding deletion requests must not sit
inside the partner's interest. Retention, by default: at graduation your team repo —
history included — is transferred or forked to you, so you always leave with your own
notebook. The program keeps its copy too: the org retains team repos, raw logs included,
as the program's archive — access stays limited to the practitioner team and the
steward, and every use stays inside §1's list, so future cohorts can learn from real
trajectories under the same rules that protect yours. Deletion stays yours to ask for,
at any time, cohort running or long over: a request to the data steward removes your raw
logs from the archive, witnessed by a dated issue (summaries and commit metadata may
remain in the cohort record; checkpoint pins then remain as commit identifiers — the
record of what was signed, not your bytes). Your cohort may override either default by PR
before enrollment — a default you can read beats a blank we meant to fill.

## 2. What we grade — and what we never grade

**Graded:** the trajectory of your agent-driving over the course, the honesty and quality
of your reflection, and your verification practice — drawn from the sessions you cite in
your sprint reports. The criteria are public: `curriculum/rubric.md` in your cohort repo.
Sprint-01 is graded formatively only (rubric v1).

**Never graded:** session count, dead ends, naive prompts, abandoned approaches.

Also never graded: writing quality, summary length, which language you write in, and how
often you ask for help. Reflection is graded for honesty and specificity, never polish —
two honest sentences beat a page. Write in English or Ukrainian, whichever lets you think;
practitioners read both. Also never graded: the contents of your report's "What we
understand now" lines — they calibrate teaching and are never evidence for or against a
grade (see rubric standing rule 4).

**An honestly documented failure can only help your grade, never hurt it. Session count is
not a metric.**

## 3. What practitioners owe you

- Within **48 hours of the checkpoint merge** (the G2 sign-off on the dated checkpoints
  in your cohort's sprint plan — other Saturdays are heartbeat pin-refreshes: nothing is
  due and no note is owed): a short keep/change/try note committed to your repo at
  `reports/feedback/sprint-NN.md`.
- Every **"Blocked / need"** ask answered or explicitly deferred — never silently dropped.
- Answers to your **Blocked / need** asks land in the cohort digest (cohort repo,
  `reports/sprint-NN/digest.md`) — start your next report by reading it.
- Praise in public, coaching in private.
- We read what we ask you to write.
- If anything in this section is not kept, escalate to the data steward / course lead
  named in your cohort repo's README — never to the practitioner involved; every
  acknowledged breach is answered in the next digest's Program notes.
- Practitioners get the same wartime clause you do: a disrupted practitioner flags it in
  the digest's Program notes, and the note is owed within 48h of the flag clearing —
  disrupted, never silently dropped.

## 4. What agents may do

- **Propose, never apply.**
- Draft the factual part of a summary — never your "Dead ends / friction" or "Lesson" lines.
- Describe work, never workers.

## 5. End of cohort & wartime grace

You leave with this repo and your logs: a fork or transfer window is guaranteed before any
team deletion. The org keeps the pinned checkpoint snapshots and the program archive under
§1's retention mechanics — practitioner-and-steward access only, §1's use-list only, and
your deletion request always stands.

Blackout or air-raid disruption: flag it in your sprint report's Disruptions line (Slack
works for the news; the report line is the record), and a practitioner re-runs the pin
for your team. It is recorded as *disrupted*, never *stalled* — no penalty, and no
justification owed.
